A-OK Blind Bridge

Local control of 433 MHz A-OK blind motors (sold as Motolux and under other names) from Home Assistant, using an ESP32 and a CC1101 radio. No cloud, no hub.

Install

  1. Wire the CC1101 to the ESP32 as shown below.
  2. Plug the ESP32 into this computer with a USB data cable.
  3. Press Install, pick the serial port, then enter your Wi-Fi when asked.
Your browser can't flash devices. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open this page over https to install.

If it can't connect, hold the BOOT button on the ESP32 while you press Install. On Windows you may need the CP210x or CH340 USB driver.

Wiring

Power the CC1101 from 3V3, never 5V.

CC1101ESP32 (HW-395 / DevKit)
1 GNDGND
2 VCC3V3
3 GDO0GPIO 12
4 CSNGPIO 5
5 SCKGPIO 18
6 MOSIGPIO 23
7 MISOGPIO 19
8 GDO2GPIO 27

After installing

  1. Open http://aok-blinds.local (or the address the installer shows).
  2. Remote: press Start learning, then Up, Down and Stop on your hand remote.
  3. Set up blinds: add one blind per remote channel.
  4. Calibrate: time each blind opening and closing.
  5. Settings → Home Assistant: enter your MQTT broker. The blinds appear in Home Assistant by themselves.

Prefer ESPHome? See the ESPHome component. Source, docs and protocol notes are on GitHub.
